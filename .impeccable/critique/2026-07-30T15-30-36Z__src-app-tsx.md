# The Annex — full game design, scenario, ambience, and NPC review

## Executive verdict

The Annex is now a **premium 3D narrative vertical slice**, not a 2.5D mockup. Its bounded
Three.js hubs, cinematic HUD, authored close-read rooms, phase chambers, responsive
controls, and deterministic legal state form a credible game. Case 81's deposition proves
the project can deliver genuinely interactive dramatic writing.

It is **not yet an AAA-feeling playable drama or immersive playground**. The visual layer
is ahead of the scenario layer: investigation can be bypassed, opening approaches promise
routes they do not create, trust mostly annotates rather than changes encounters, and most
characters comment after decisions instead of acting before them. The fastest path upward
is to make the existing two cases causally and emotionally complete before adding another
case or another visual system.

Current whole-slice assessment: **7/10**.

## What is strongest

### 1. The world finally has its own identity

Case 77's rain, wet civic materials, carrier rail, audit mirror, acoustic corridor, and
compressed archive feel authored rather than borrowed from BioShock or Cyberpunk. Case 81
uses dry dust, long light, a restrained hum, and procedural shutters to create a different
pressure. The cinematic HUD belongs to the auditor's work rather than floating over it as
a website layer.

The 3D hubs are real and usable. Portal focus, camera travel, fallback posters, semantic
controls, and the same state across visual and accessible paths are strong production
decisions. This is now an authored diorama game, not a fake parallax title screen.

### 2. The cases form an excellent conceptual inversion

Case 77 asks whether memory can continue a person. Case 81 asks whether an institution can
manufacture the witness it needs without also creating a person it must respect. The
tribunals avoid a golden answer and name the actual costs: debt, estate, confinement,
personhood, testimony use, and office-level liability.

Case 81's four-stage deposition—**Identity → Terms → Use → File**—is the current high
point. Ellis may self-name, the auditor may impose a designation or verify office without
using it as a name, disclosure may stop at the office or move under protected terms, and
permission to use the recording is asked separately. Refusal survives later
certification. This is procedural writing performed as play, not merely displayed as
prose.

### 3. The Fourth Margin is a disciplined Easter-egg system

The Nietzsche and Schopenhauer fragments are attributed and paired with original
counter-readings instead of being treated as the game's answer. Reader Key 04 opens a
genuinely hidden 3D space with three independent objects, an always-available exit, no
secret best ending, and no contamination of evidence or legal state.

The repaired gardening guide, transit stubs, lunch paper, blue thread, and coriander seed
are among the game's best human details. The Unnumbered Reading Room succeeds as optional
marginalia. It does **not** yet create nonlinear scenario substance because its choices
are deliberately view-local and write no run history; that is the correct boundary for
an Easter egg, but it should not be counted as a side quest or extra level.

## Priority findings

### [P1] Investigation can be bypassed while the lattice reveals unearned facts

In a clean live run, reconstruction opened after only Care Ward. The lattice displayed
the full text of all four anchors, including facts tied to unvisited sites. Two anchors
explicitly labelled “Not corroborated by your field route” were selectable and produced
**Emergent personhood**, which was added as evidence. After one additional site, the
tribunal opened at 2/4 sites and all three lawful findings became available.

This is not just permissive role-play. It makes investigation advisory and lets the
reconstruction screen spoil discoveries the player never made. The reducer opens the
lattice after one completed site, accepts any two fragments, and uses corroboration only
to select event tone. Tribunal access is simply two sites plus any reconstruction.

Relevant seams:

- `canEnterTribunal`: `src/game/engine.ts:243`
- lattice opens after one site: `src/game/engine.ts:456`
- any pair is accepted and added as evidence: `src/game/engine.ts:489`
- every fragment's full content remains visible: `src/components/Reconstruction.tsx:91`

Recommended fix:

1. Give fragments three explicit states: **unknown**, **known but unsupported**, and
   **corroborated**.
2. Unknown anchors may show a timecode/source silhouette, but not their decisive content.
3. Allow at most one known-but-unsupported anchor in a model, with an explicit
   “speculative filing” acknowledgement and a mandatory tribunal objection.
4. Keep every lawful verdict available; change the hearing quality and cost rather than
   hiding a “good” ending behind evidence collection.
5. Correct “Every pairing produces a different valid model.” Six possible pairs collapse
   into four models in both cases. Use “Every pair produces a valid, incomplete model.”

### [P1] Opening approach and social memory promise more playable divergence than exists

“Begin with the person,” “Begin outside permission,” and similar choices seed one trust
point and a method tag, but the investigation view selects the first incomplete site.
Case 77's Care approach therefore opens with Registry Intake selected rather than Care
Ward; the covert approach does not route the camera or encounter to Maintenance. The
approach schema has no starting-site or opening-encounter field.

Trust then drives stance labels, reaction copy, and some debrief variants. It does not
normally make an NPC volunteer corroboration, withhold access, change a scene, alter
testimony, or support/object differently in the room. Cross-run memory compresses only
the immediately previous run to ±1 and a generic method sentence.

Relevant seams:

- approach data contains no route: `src/game/types.ts:264`
- investigation selects the first incomplete site: `src/components/Investigation.tsx:226`
- prior trust is compressed to ±1: `src/game/engine.ts:194`
- verdict validation does not read trust, alarm, or evidence: `src/game/engine.ts:550`

Recommended fix:

1. Add an authored `suggestedSiteId` or short opening encounter to each approach and move
   the camera/selection there without removing player freedom.
2. Make trust change **who acts**, not which moral ending unlocks. A trusted presence may
   volunteer one corroboration or hearing support; an alienated one files the objection.
3. Author one field-level precedent effect per case, using the existing “prior forged seal
   is watched in Case 81” variation as the model.
4. Let the Mirror infer one pattern across multiple runs instead of repeating only the
   latest verdict.

### [P1] The subject characters and recurring cast do not yet carry the drama

77-A can be heard or pressure-tested, and a skipped encounter is correctly named as an
omission at tribunal. She still has no present-tense request, temporary preferred name,
ordinary desire, or pre-verdict hearing voice. Her strongest wants arrive only after the
auditor has assigned her name, estate, movement, and legal relationships.

Ellis is substantially better because the deposition lets them name, limit, consent,
refuse, and later object. Even Ellis lacks an ordinary life detail or living relationship
unrelated to proving the case.

The Registrar, Shepherd, Defector, and Small Archivist have clear vocabulary but are
defined as role + principle + portrait. They mostly speak polished judgments after the
reducer has already committed the action. No two recurring characters truly confront
one another, and city/claimant counsel remain unnamed positions represented by empty
chairs.

Recommended deterministic encounters:

1. **77-A, “for the next hour.”** Before tribunal, derive one temporary address request
   from subject-contact state: Mara, 77-A, or leave the name blank. Add one ordinary want,
   such as the burnt toast the ward discards. It is not evidence and does not dictate a
   verdict.
2. **Ellis packet correction.** Between Identity and Terms, Ellis corrects one mundane
   error and names one Lower Span relationship. Derive the variant from the existing
   identity choice; no new hidden morality score.
3. **Two presences in one corridor.** After two sites, choose a persona pair from the
   completed actions. Higher trust offers hearing support; lower trust objects. Let them
   address each other once.
4. **Both counsel chairs occupied.** Name the city and claimant advocates and give each a
   relationship to Ellis or a claimant. Preserve the current legal positions.
5. **The body receives the ruling.** Insert a 15–30 second view-local tableau before the
   written debrief: a name card, ward gate, admissibility shutter, empty chair, or trace
   scanner selected from canonical outcome state.

### [P2] Bespoke visual rituals still resolve to a repetitive scenario grammar

Registry's carrier rail, Maintenance's acoustic shadow, the Small Archive mechanism, and
the visual site plates give the rooms flavor. Canonically, most locations still end in
one of two mutually exclusive field actions followed by nonbranching staged lines. Only
the deposition asks multiple consequential questions.

One Care Ward filing required method select, select again to file, six Continue presses,
Close the beat, and Close the record—roughly ten interactions for a predetermined
outcome. That pacing feels cinematic the first time and laborious on replay.

Recommended fix:

- Give every site one unique consequential verb, using the deposition as the quality bar.
- Make at least one site result revise the next site's opening state.
- Add one midpoint collision in each case that changes the interpretation of an earlier
  fact.
- Keep double-confirmation for irreversible commits, but collapse passive consequence
  playback to one or two advances.
- Add **skip seen beat / fast transcript / auto-advance** for later runs.

### [P2] Ambience is visually premium but sonically and performatively prototype-grade

The current audio engine is technically disciplined and spatially state-aware, but it is
filtered Web Audio noise and oscillators only. There are no authored portal-specific
foley assets, performed dialogue, material impacts, room actions, or musical transitions.
The hubs are atmospheric but largely uninhabited. 77-A remains a static obscured
silhouette; Case 81's Lab, Records, and Counsel rooms are beautiful empty plates.

Recommended production order:

1. Record site-signature foley first: carrier latch, archive drawer, rain infrastructure,
   acoustic gate, recorder shutter, seal mechanism.
2. Add restrained voice performance for 77-A and Ellis before voicing the entire cast.
3. Add small canonical physical reactions—breath, hand shift, chair turn, shutter closure—
   instead of broad morality-colored lighting.
4. Let each committed method leave one visual or acoustic scar on return.
5. Turn reconstruction into a 10–20 second audiovisual collision of the two selected
   anchors.

At 390×844, the HUD remained collision-free with 44–48px targets. At the supported
320×568 short portrait, the dialogue and prompt trays overlap by 1.5px and occupy nearly
the whole world window. Add a compact-height portrait rule that removes secondary HUD
copy or reserves a clear interaction band.

## Case-by-case review

### Case 77 — The Vale Continuity Claim

What works:

- The stronger environmental identity: impossible rain, wet civic archive, carrier rail,
  acoustic omission, and shelf-zero taxonomy.
- The underlying dilemma is excellent and the verdict costs are unusually concrete.
- Registry + Maintenance can establish one real authority-family collision.
- Skipped/pressured subject contact is surfaced at tribunal rather than silently ignored.

What is missing:

- 77-A is still more case object than person.
- Site order rarely revises later scenes.
- The lattice leaks the most consequential anchor content.
- The tribunal is visually strong but lacks 77-A's presence.
- The most emotional material is delivered after commitment in the debrief.

### Case 81 — The Commissioned Witness

What works:

- The deposition is a genuinely distinct game grammar.
- Identity, disclosure, consent, admissibility, personhood, and testimony are correctly
  separated.
- The tribunal's dual legal channels are clear and consequential.
- A skipped deposition never manufactures testimony.
- Case 77 precedent copy and the watched-forgery field effect are good campaign memory.

What is missing:

- Lab, Records, and Counsel do not match the deposition's dramatic specificity.
- Counsel is disembodied despite being the obvious ensemble scene.
- The filed site summary “capture is verified; its use is not” can blur Ellis's granted
  permission with tribunal admissibility. Show both labels explicitly.
- The second dormant-authority fraud route begins to feel like a house formula rather
  than a case-specific surprise.

### The Fourth Margin and Unnumbered Reading Room

The Easter-egg chain is one of the cleanest systems in the build. It is optional,
accessible, provenance-documented, persistent, and never a secret superior ending. Keep
its density low.

Do not add more philosopher quotations just to make the room richer. Add ordinary objects,
repairs, mistakes, food, habits, and human handwriting. If the goal is a genuinely
nonlinear side level, author that as an engine-owned side investigation with a bounded
fact or relationship consequence; do not overload marginalia with evidence or rewards.

The voices use distinct nouns, but cadence converges on balanced antithesis, em-dash
reversals, “X proves A, not B,” and “You filed…” judgments. Protect one standout
aphoristic line per case. Surround it with correction, hesitation, unfinished sentences,
mundane speech, disagreement, humor, and silence.

## What “AAA” should mean here

The project should not chase an open world or polygon count. Its achievable premium
identity is a **fixed-camera or bounded-camera civic-noir immersive drama** with:

- bespoke interaction grammar in every major room;
- subjects who act before and after the ruling;
- named advocates and inter-character conflict;
- authored foley, spatial mix, and selective voice performance;
- environments that remember filed actions;
- audiovisual reconstructions and immediate aftermath vignettes;
- deterministic route variants and precedent effects;
- a fast replay mode.

The current Three.js world is an excellent spatial shell. It is not yet a playground:
there is no free locomotion, systemic object manipulation, NPC navigation, physical
inventory, or emergent stealth. Decide explicitly whether the target is:

1. a bounded authored immersive drama, which fits the current architecture; or
2. a free-walk 3D immersive sim, which is a materially larger game and engine scope.

For this project, the bounded authored drama is the stronger and more achievable identity.

## Persona red flags

**Jordan, first-time narrative player:** “Begin with the person” appears to set a route,
but the view selects Registry first. Jordan can then read and file unsupported anchor
facts, so they may not understand why the four rooms matter.

**Alex, replay-focused player:** Counterfactuals and precedent promise replay depth, but
most route changes are copy and the passive Continue/Close cadence is slow once seen.

**Sam, accessibility-focused player:** Skip link, named regions, focus transfer,
reduced-motion handling, contrast modes, native controls, and 44–48px targets are strong.
The 320×568 layout needs a compact-height mode so dialogue does not erase the spatial
scene.

**A character-driven RPG player:** The interface says “social memory,” but the cast rarely
offers, withholds, interrupts, or changes a scene because of relationship state.

## Minor observations

- “Every pairing produces a different valid model” is factually misleading; both cases
  map six pairs to four models.
- The game is not generic cyberpunk. Its strongest identity is municipal civic noir; keep
  avoiding neon spectacle and franchise imitation.
- Case 81's consent/admissibility terminology is sophisticated enough to deserve two
  separate visible labels wherever the deposition is summarized.
- The illicit “dormant authority” ending should not automatically recur in every case.
- Generated-asset provenance is unusually thorough; commercial production still needs
  the recorded human and rights review noted in the provenance file.
