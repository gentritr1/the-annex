# The Annex — Game & Content Brief, Story Audit, and Campaign Roadmap

> Editorial working document · 30 July 2026
>
> Scope: the complete playable game in brief, the two current cases, characters, campaign mystery, ambience, narrative gaps, and a practical path for future cases.

## How to use this document

This is a workshop document, not a replacement for the canonical authored content in
`src/game/cases/case77.ts`, `src/game/cases/case81.ts`, and `src/game/content.ts`.

- **Current** sections summarize what is already playable.
- **Proposed** sections are editorial recommendations, not established canon.
- **Draft lines** are voice tests and starting points, not final dialogue.
- The goal is not to imitate *BioShock* or *Cyberpunk 2077*. The useful lesson is their
  integration of place, character, interface, sound, and story. The Annex should keep its
  own civic-noir identity: no generic neon city, no lore pasted over empty spectacle.

## 1. Executive editorial verdict

The Annex already has a rare and valuable foundation: **procedure is both the gameplay
and the moral argument**. The player does not merely discover what a person is; the
player's methods help create the legal reality later presented as objective.

The two current cases are intellectually strong, visually coherent, and unusually rich
in lines worth remembering. Case 77 has the more complete environmental identity. Case 81
has the more dramatic central scene. Together they form an excellent conceptual
inversion:

- Case 77 asks whether recovered memory can continue a person.
- Case 81 asks whether an institution can manufacture the witness it needs without also
  creating a person it must respect.

The largest risk is not a weak premise. It is **dramatic sameness beneath excellent
prose**. Both cases use four locations, two methods per location, four anchors, a
two-anchor reconstruction, a tribunal, a review option, and an illicit certification
route. Case 81's deposition is the one sequence that truly changes the interaction
grammar.

The writing also risks becoming uniformly aphoristic. Many lines are excellent, but if
every character speaks in polished conclusions, the voices begin to merge. The target
should be **dense insight, not continuous slogans**: concrete behavior, awkwardness,
silence, ordinary details, and disagreement should make the signature lines hit harder.

### What is strongest now

- A distinctive player fantasy: civic auditor as author of legal reality.
- Two excellent philosophical premises with no simple good/evil answer.
- The fourth-minute campaign mystery and the Continuity Directorate hook.
- A deterministic, replayable record of method, trust, alarm, evidence, and precedent.
- Four memorable ideological companions and a Mirror that contaminates replay with memory.
- Case 77's rain ledger, bounded 3D concourse, thresholds, materials, and spatial sound.
- Case 81's Ellis deposition, consent theme, refusal staging, dust, light, and silence.
- Fully authored reconstruction-versus-verdict tension lines.
- Strong debriefs that show counterfactuals instead of declaring a correct ending.

### What most needs work

1. Mara/77-A and Ellis need fuller lives, present-tense desires, and agency outside being
   evidence.
2. The locations need to form an escalating causal investigation, not four thematic
   spokes around the same question.
3. Case 81 now owns a distinct two-question deposition and dual-channel hearing; its
   Lab, Records, and Counsel sites still largely return to Case 77's binary spoke grammar.
4. The implemented hearing signals and objections make method visible before the debrief,
   but trust and most non-deposition evidence still need more human response in the room.
5. A skipped Case 81 deposition must remain visibly absent in every legal-channel label;
   certification may authorize an account that exists, never create one.
6. Cases 77 and 81 now pay off bounded fourth-minute facts. Case 84 must turn those facts
   into one concrete claimant consequence rather than another deferred mystery.
7. The shared cast need personal complicities, evolving relationships, and conflicts with
   one another.
8. Case 81 now has bounded spatial HUD and ambience parity with Case 77; remaining
   environment work should deepen authored site reactions and aftermath rather than
   rebuild that foundation.
9. The campaign now persists six compact authored outcome facts. A playable Case 84 must
   snapshot the imported values at case start so later replays do not rewrite a completed
   Case 84 record.
10. “AAA” should mean authored response, performance, sound, continuity, and consequence—
    not simply more polygons or a larger map.
11. The auditor is almost entirely disembodied. Future cases should reveal why this person
    holds the office, what authority they can lose, and why the Mirror is attached to their
    record—without imposing a fixed personality on the player.

### North-star statement

> The Annex is an intimate civic-noir immersive drama in which every room is an argument,
> every procedure leaves a scar, and every ruling saves one truth by making another
> harder to see.

## 2. The complete game in brief

### Player fantasy

The player is a single auditor operating inside a rain-dark municipal archive. A case
arrives with an impossible legal question. The city asks for a ruling before the facts,
people, and categories can be made clean. The player chooses how to investigate, files a
model from incomplete anchors, and issues a finding that changes legal reality.

The game remembers not only the result, but the method used to reach it.

### Core play loop

| Phase | What the player does | What the phase means |
|---|---|---|
| Briefing | Reads the public record, sees prior Mirror residue, and chooses an opening approach | Declares where the record begins |
| Investigation | Visits four sites and commits one mutually exclusive method at each visited site | Turns conduct into evidence |
| Reconstruction | Selects exactly two of four memory anchors | Files a theory that is coherent but never complete |
| Tribunal | Reviews contradictions and ratifies a legal finding | Converts interpretation into civic reality |
| Debrief | Sees consequences, refusals, persona responses, and the fourth-minute revelation | Shows what the ruling protected, erased, or deferred |
| Replay / next case | Carries precedent and method residue forward | Makes the next audit inherit the previous one |

Current pacing permits the tribunal after at least two sites and one reconstruction. That
keeps a run compact, but it also allows the player to omit the person most affected by the
ruling. That omission can be powerful if the game confronts it explicitly.

### Canonical systems

- **Opening approaches:** Procedure, Care, Covert access, and Inquiry/Curiosity.
- **Method vocabulary:** Procedure, Negotiation, Stealth, Systems, Reconstruction,
  Nonviolent, Fraud, Care, and Coercion.
- **Evidence:** Authored facts with provenance, status, and reactions.
- **Trust:** Four recurring personas remember how the player worked.
- **Alarm:** Illicit actions leave civic pressure and can alter later actions.
- **Reconstruction:** Any two anchors resolve deterministically to an authored model.
- **Contradiction:** Every model and verdict combination has a specific tension line.
- **Counterfactuals:** The debrief names the sibling method the player refused.
- **Precedent:** Case 77's verdict and individual/general scope are cited in Case 81; one
  illicit precedent changes a later field action.
- **Compact outcomes:** Three Case 77 facts and three Case 81 facts are validated and
  committed atomically with the verdict.
- **Case-specific hearing verbs:** Case 77 sets precedent scope; Case 81 resolves
  personhood and testimony use through separate legal channels.
- **Provisional deposition:** Case 81 records identity, disclosure terms, exact use
  request, and stable testimony-use outcome without confusing raw capture with admission.
- **The Fourth Margin:** Optional, persistent quotation fragments sit beside the legal
  record, carry original in-world counter-readings, and can assemble Reader Key 04 without
  changing evidence, trust, alarm, access, testimony, outcomes, or findings.
- **Accessibility:** Preferences persist independently of case progress; ambient audio is
  opt-in; reduced motion has authored static behavior.

### Recurring cast

| Character | Current role and principle | Dramatic opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| **The Registrar** | Custodian of legal continuity. “A person is a chain the city can verify.” | Reveal what they previously signed, protected, or erased; make procedure a costly form of care rather than a stock conservative position |
| **The Shepherd** | Care-ward advocate. “Care recognizes a self before procedure does.” | Show how care can become possessive, coercive, or biased; give the Shepherd a relationship that complicates their certainty |
| **The Defector** | Compromised systems guide. “Every clean record is hiding who paid for it.” | Make their access possible because of personal complicity; force them to choose between exposure and protecting someone they harmed |
| **The Small Archivist** | Collector of missing categories. “What you refuse to name still becomes evidence.” | Let her act, risk custody, make mistakes, and preserve chosen names; avoid using her only as an oracle who speaks in perfect riddles |
| **The Mirror** | Replays prior findings and method residue | Define its limits and agenda. It should infer from the player's record, not sound omniscient |
| **The city** | Archive, law, care system, tribunal, and Continuity Directorate | Gradually embody it through named people with defensible motives so “the institution” does not remain an abstract villain |

### Current campaign spine

1. The Lower Span archive collapses.
2. A forbidden process reconstructs Mara Vale as 77-A eleven days later.
3. Records show a checksum signed in the **fourth minute after the collapse**, after the
   archive it supposedly certifies was already gone.
4. A corridor minute was excluded by policy, not accidentally lost.
5. Earlier composite persons were removed from public taxonomy.
6. The city later constructs Ellis Marne / 81-C as a witness to the same collapse.
7. Ellis identifies the **Continuity Directorate's seal** as the authority that excised
   the fourth minute.
8. Ellis saw the individual hand carrying the seal. Case 81 may record voluntary,
   protected, refused, unasked, compelled, or tainted office-level use, but no Case 81 path
   identifies the hand.
9. The authored hook says Case 84 begins with the unresolved conflict between **person and
   office**.

This is a strong mystery because it joins the metaphysical question to an institutional
crime. The next step is to show why the fourth minute mattered to living people, not only
to the archive.

## 3. Case 77 — The Vale Continuity Claim

### Current dossier

- **Code:** CMA–77–A
- **Subject:** Mara Vale / restoration instance 77-A
- **Question:** Does a restored person inherit the identity that supplied their memories?
- **Public record:** Mara died in the Lower Span archive collapse. Eleven days later,
  77-A was assembled from civic backups, care records, and private memory fragments.
- **Mandate:** Certify continuity, charter a new person, or preserve 77-A under protected
  review.
- **Core dramatic truth:** The city cannot determine identity without authoring the
  identity it later claims merely to recognize.

### Current investigation content

| Location | Two final methods | Evidence and dramatic meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Registry Intake** | Authenticate the custody chain / Trace the checksum past closure | A clean chain proves provenance, not identity; the “original” record was signed in the fourth minute after the original archive was gone |
| **Care Ward 12** | Let 77-A tell one memory uninterrupted / Pressure-test the contradiction | She remembers rain against a window in a room with no exterior wall; she feels a scar her present tissue never carried |
| **Maintenance Spine** | Walk the acoustic shadow / Forge maintenance authority | The fourth minute was excluded by policy; a dormant credential can write continuity without a tribunal vote |
| **The Small Archive** | Answer the question the statute avoids / Seal the prohibited category index | The law has no category for the person 77-A became while adults argued about Mara; earlier composites were recorded and then erased from taxonomy |

The special room interactions are thematically excellent:

- Registry uses three admitted carriers, one rejected carrier, and an audit mirror that
  accepts the refused fourth-minute mark.
- Care Ward places 77-A against impossible reflected rain and makes listening or pressure
  physically legible.
- Maintenance turns sensor omission into a rain-masked acoustic traversal.
- Small Archive makes category failure tactile through question cards, shelf zero, and
  removal slips.

### Reconstruction

The four anchors are:

1. **Embodied echo:** the scar sensation in an unscarred body.
2. **Recognition:** the Shepherd's fear response and recognition of Mara.
3. **Signed continuity:** the institutional hash chain.
4. **The first new memory:** a post-restoration dream supplied by no donor.

Every pair resolves to one of four models:

- Relational continuity.
- Institutional continuity.
- Emergent personhood.
- Irreducible composite.

### Tribunal findings

- **Certify Mara Vale as continuous:** 77-A inherits Mara's name, obligations, property,
  and legal relationships.
- **Charter 77-A as a new person:** 77-A receives full protection while Mara's former
  legal identity remains closed.
- **Order protected review:** 77-A is preserved for ninety days while an independent
  review continues.
- **Write continuity without a vote:** the dormant credential makes Mara's status
  canonical through fraud.

Before any finding can be filed, the player must set its legal reach:

- **Bind 77-A only:** this ruling resolves one claim and supplies analogy, not a general
  continuity rule.
- **Set general continuity precedent:** later restorations may invoke the rule together
  with every gap and method attached to it.

One live objection names the most consequential route defect before that scope is chosen.
The exact `trace-checksum` plus `walk-acoustic-shadow` route proves only that the late
Registry mark and excluded Maintenance minute share an authority family. It does not name
the Directorate, the hand, motive, or mechanism.

All sixteen reconstruction-versus-finding combinations have authored contradictions.
The completed run also exports `valeContact`, `authorityLink77`, and `continuityScope` as
validated campaign facts. This is one of the strongest pieces of the current game.

### Strongest current material

- Provenance is not identity.
- Recognition can preserve a self or validate a performance.
- Care can reveal truth, and truth can be extracted through harm.
- Missing categories are institutional decisions.
- Delay distributes harm rather than avoiding it.
- Humane ends can be corrupted by fraudulent means.
- Every verdict protects something by making something else legally invisible.

Protect these **ideas**, but reserve the aphorism slot for the impossible rain/window
knowledge. The other sentences are an editorial bank, not a requirement to surface all of
them in one run:

> “Your route through the record will become part of the record.”

> “A valid chain proves provenance, not continuity.”

> “There was no window. Write down that I know that.”

> “Mara would have told you to stop. I didn't. Decide what that means.”

> “A whole person, folded to fit the drawer.”

> “The city becomes the author of the continuity it claims merely to verify.”

> “Protection that removes a person from life is still a kind of removal.”

> “Adults call a shelf empty when they removed the label themselves.”

### Main gaps

1. **77-A is more dilemma than person.** The player can decide her name, property,
   relationships, and confinement without learning what she wants before judgment.
2. **The mystery payoff is bounded but not yet human.** The exact Registry/Maintenance
   route proves deliberate authority-family coordination, but Case 77 still cannot say
   what the coordinated act cost a living claimant.
3. **The sites do not escalate enough.** They are powerful thematic spokes, but the
   discoveries rarely collide or revise one another as the player changes order.
4. **Opening approaches promise more divergence than they deliver.** They alter state and
   framing, but not enough access, follow-up, scene order, or companion presence.
5. **The “humane” and “harsh” methods can be too easy to identify.** Procedure needs
   indispensable protective value; care and uncertainty need credible risks.
6. **Investigation can feel advisory.** Lawful verdicts remain available regardless of
   evidence, trust gates little, and uncorroborated anchors remain selectable.
7. **The tribunal remains presentation-light.** It now has a route-derived objection and
   a consequential scope choice, but 77-A is not physically present and makes no final
   request.
8. **The best emotional consequences arrive after the decision.**
9. **Some rituals use many clicks for one predetermined insight.**
10. **The recurring cast act as philosophical offices before they act as people.**

### Implemented lean arc and remaining additions

This can deepen the case without turning a 15–25 minute run into a feature-length chapter.

1. **Still needed — 77-A names the immediate stake.** Keep her visibly available without
   removing the omission route. In a voluntary short exchange, let her state how she wants
   to be addressed for the next hour and one ordinary thing she wants after the hearing.
   This is explicitly not proof of identity.
2. **Implemented — investigation establishes method.** Preserve the existing
   opening approach and first room.
3. **Implemented — one factual collision.** Only `trace-checksum` plus
   `walk-acoustic-shadow` establishes that the late Registry mark and excluded Maintenance
   minute share an authority family. No other two-site route receives that fact.
4. **Still needed — 77-A interprets the new danger.** She learns prior composites were
   erased and chooses whether to claim Mara's name, reject it, or leave the question open.
   Her preference should complicate, not dictate, the verdict.
5. **Implemented — hearing action has legal force.** A route-derived challenger names the
   strongest gap, then the player chooses individual or general precedent scope.
6. **Still needed — restrained subject presence.** Let 77-A answer or refuse without
   adding a universal response menu or filing minigame.
7. **Still needed — short consequence image.** Use a 15–30 second release gate, naming
   desk, sealed ward window, or fraudulent registry scan before the concise debrief.

### Proposed choice consequence without hard-locking verdicts

Do not turn the tribunal into “collect the right evidence to unlock the good ending.”
Keep all lawful findings available. Change the **quality and cost of the hearing**:

- A trusted persona volunteers corroboration; an alienated one files an objection.
- An uncorroborated model can be used, but the player must acknowledge its unsupported
  anchor in the record.
- Skipping Care Ward means 77-A is heard for the first time only after the player has
  already modeled her.
- Sealing the index preserves precedent but prevents the Archivist from naming a prior
  composite during the hearing.
- Forgery can solve the immediate status while creating a visible trace that follows the
  player into every later case.

### Proposed ambience arc

| Space | Current strength | Add next |
|---|---|---|
| Concourse | Bounded 3D hub, rain, civic brutalism, four acoustic thresholds | Let water pressure, announcements, staff shadows, and routing lights change after each filing |
| Registry | Excellent carrier-and-mirror ritual | Add human handling traces and one delayed impossible sound/reflection from Mirror Mark 04 |
| Care Ward | Strong impossible-rain image and moral staging | Let breath, hand movement, lamp, and reflection respond to listening or pressure without requiring facial animation |
| Maintenance | Rain is both infrastructure and stealth system | Make acoustic occlusion spatially decisive; let the Defector's broken comms reveal personal guilt |
| Small Archive | Original category mechanic and hand-worn world | Persist a chosen classification, an erased name, or a newly occupied shelf across the run |
| Reconstruction | Strong intellectual pairing | Make each model an audiovisual collision of the selected memories, not only a card result |
| Tribunal | Strong legal framing | Add exhibit placement, chamber acoustics, objections, and 77-A's presence |
| Aftermath | Strong prose consequences | Add a sixty-to-ninety-second playable environmental consequence |

## 4. Case 81 — The Commissioned Witness

### Current dossier

- **Code:** CMA–81–C
- **Subject:** Restoration instance 81-C / former Deputy Registrar Ellis Marne.
- **Question:** Can the city restore the witness it needs and still call the restoration
  a person?
- **Public record:** The city assembled 81-C from institutional backups to testify about
  the Lower Span collapse. A raw recorder may preserve an account before judgment, but
  legal use remains shuttered until the tribunal rules—or a forged seal forces both
  channels open.
- **Mandate:** Decide whether 81-C is a witness, a document the city is speaking through,
  or a standing that cannot yet be certified.
- **Core dramatic truth:** The city may have created a person while trying to manufacture
  evidence.

### Current investigation content

| Location | Two final methods | Evidence and dramatic meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Deposition Suite** | Take the sworn statement / Cross-examine the witness | Compliance does not prove standing; divergence may indicate a person or a failed instrument |
| **Restoration Lab** | Audit the restoration log / Replicate the memory seed | The city closed assembly in the fourth minute; a reseeded fragment produces a memory no donor supplied |
| **Records Annex** | Pull the service record / Forge the certification seal | The record ties Ellis to the office seeking certification; a retired seal can create standing without a vote |
| **Counsel Office** | Brief city counsel / Depose opposing counsel | The city needs the only surviving account; opposition protects the record from a commissioned instrument while risking erasure of a person |

### The deposition

The deposition is the current narrative high point and Case 81's distinct interaction
grammar. A raw recorder may preserve an account before certification; recording never
makes the account admissible. The playable sequence has four visible stages—**Identity,
Terms, Use, File**—built from two decisive questions and one exact use request:

1. **Identity and office:** let Ellis name the speaker, impose “81-C,” or verify Deputy
   Registrar service without using the office as a name.
2. **Office, hand, and terms:** accept office-level disclosure, demand the individual
   hand, or offer protected terms for possible later disclosure.
3. **Legal use:** ask “May this recorded account be used in this proceeding?” or close the
   raw record without asking.
4. **File:** preserve the resulting use boundary with the account.

The answer is authored from stable terms, not from the entry action or a hidden virtue
score:

- no request produces `unasked`;
- an accepted office-level request produces `voluntary-office`;
- protected terms produce `protected-hand`, meaning an offer—not an identified hand;
- demanding the hand produces `refused`;
- imposing “81-C” and demanding the hand produces `compelled`;
- a forged certification may later produce `tainted`, but never rewrites refusal or
  compulsion as consent.

Certification and consent therefore remain separate facts. A tribunal can make an
existing office-level account legally admissible over refusal while the refusal remains
attached to the record. Skipping Ellis remains a real route: no account exists, no use can
be authorized, and no ruling may manufacture testimony.

The one protected standout line is:

> “I remember the order the lights failed in. The record does not.”

Other Ellis lines should function as exact corrections, permissions, refusals, or limits
rather than compete with it as additional slogans.

### Reconstruction

The four anchors are:

1. **Oath cadence.**
2. **Redacted clause.**
3. **Seed signature.**
4. **Unscripted answer.**

Their pairings resolve to:

- Testimonial standing.
- Procedural legitimacy.
- Fabricated witness.
- Deadlocked standing.

### Tribunal findings

- **Certify 81-C as a witness:** personhood and admissibility are granted together.
- **Reject the witness's standing:** personhood is denied and the testimony is struck.
- **Seat under provisional standing:** Ellis and the account remain suspended.
- **Recognize Ellis Marne as a person:** the commission is ended, the testimony is
  struck, and Ellis decides independently whether to speak.
- **Certify without a vote:** the dormant seal seats the witness through fraud.

The hearing displays two separate legal channels—**personhood** and **commissioned
testimony**—plus the recorded use boundary and one live objection. Static channel labels
must account for a skipped deposition: an absent account is **not taken**, not admitted,
held, struck, or forced.

All twenty reconstruction-versus-finding combinations have authored tensions. A completed
run exports:

- `testimonyUse81`: voluntary office use, protected hand offer, refused, unasked,
  compelled, tainted, or legacy unknown;
- `officeLink81`: admissible, disputed/held, absent, or legacy unknown;
- `ellisPublicStanding`: witness, person-only, provisional, rejected, forged, or legacy
  unknown.

### Strongest current material

- Personhood versus evidentiary usefulness.
- Consent versus admissibility.
- Institutional need as a form of authorship.
- Divergence as either evidence of self or failure of specification.
- Silence as an autonomous act.
- Office responsibility versus the individual hand.

Protect these **legal distinctions**, but reserve the aphorism slot for Ellis's concrete
memory/record contradiction. The other sentences are an editorial bank, not a quota:

> “The testimony is ready. The witness is not yet a person.”

> “A signed statement proves the witness complied. It does not prove the witness has
> standing to comply.”

> “Provenance of the record is not provenance of the person the record describes.”

> “A legitimate process is not a legitimate person; the city can author both.”

> “Fabrication proves a commission, not the absence of a self that survived it.”

> “A silence they chose is not one you imposed.”

> “Real to the system, void to the law.”

### Main gaps

1. **The no-account channel needs a literal visible state.** The provisional-record rule
   is now canon, but a skipped deposition must never inherit a static “admitted,” “held,”
   “struck,” or “forced” testimony label from the selected finding.
2. **The player can decide Ellis without meeting Ellis.** Only two sites are required, so
   the player can skip the deposition and still choose “Recognize Ellis.”
3. **The optional omission route needs stronger staging.** The hearing now records the
   absence, but deciding Ellis without meeting Ellis still needs the same dramatic weight
   as a recorded refusal.
4. **The collapse lacks human specificity.** We know the legal importance of the missing
   minute, but not which named people, homes, claims, or relationships were harmed.
5. **Ellis has a great voice but an incomplete life.** We need a mundane habit,
   relationship, professional compromise, private joke, shame, and present desire—none
   of which should be treated as proof of personhood.
6. **Counsel remains disembodied.** Empty chairs are excellent architecture, but the case
   needs named advocates with defensible, conflicting stakes.
7. **The deposition is distinct; the surrounding rooms are not yet.** Lab, Records, and
   Counsel remain visually rich binary menus around the stronger testimony interaction.
8. **Most prior Case 77 findings change prose but not the field investigation.**
9. **Case 81 lacks Case 77's bounded spatial world.** Its deposition tableau is excellent,
   but the wider annex still needs equivalent navigable presence and acoustics.
10. **Ellis's office/hand boundary is legally complete but dramatically austere.** Future
    content should show what preserving or violating it changes for Ellis without
    revealing the hand in Case 81.

### Implemented lean arc and remaining additions

1. **Implemented — provisional record.** The recorder runs before judgment; capture and
   admissibility are separate and visibly named.
2. **Preserved omission route.** Ellis remains optional. The hearing states when the
   auditor modeled and ruled on someone they declined to hear; future performance should
   make that absence more than a warning label.
3. **Implemented — two decisive questions.** Identity/office and office/hand/terms replace
   the earlier three-choice-beat plan. The collapse statement remains content, not a
   third optimization gate.
4. **Implemented — stable use boundary.** The exact request and authored terms resolve
   voluntary, protected, refused, unasked, compelled, or tainted use without a hidden
   relationship score.
5. **Implemented — irreversible civic cost.** Recognizing Ellis while striking the
   commissioned chain permanently removes this proceeding's route to office-level
   liability.
6. **Still needed — name the human stake.** Case 84's lean draft now specifies one narrow
   claim class and remedy; Case 81 can foreshadow it without adding a claimant ensemble.
7. **Still needed — embody the live positions.** The claimant objection is authored, but
   city and claimant counsel remain primarily interface voices rather than people in a
   room.
8. **Implemented as campaign state.** Chosen silence or disclosure now becomes a compact
   fact Case 84 can read.

### Proposed ambience arc

| Space | Current strength | Add next |
|---|---|---|
| Deposition Annex | Bounded 3D hub plus the existing Ellis diorama, dust shafts, long light sweep, and refusal state | Deepen threshold-specific sound and preserve the close-up staging as the human centerpiece |
| Deposition recorder | Strong procedural concept | Give the sealed admissibility channel a visible shutter, separate record and legal-status sounds, and a persistent state after refusal |
| Restoration Lab | Strong environment plate | Let the player tune/reseed a carrier and hear an impossible memory emerge in spatial sound |
| Records Annex | Excellent vertical authority geometry | Make descent toward the dormant seal playable while a live trace audibly closes around the player |
| Counsel Office | Equal, unresolved argument architecture | Let the player compare two retained arguments across the central aperture; introduce sparse voice presence |
| Whole annex | Dust, hum, pressure, corroboration, refusal, alarm tiers | Add portal-specific acoustics and environmental residue after every filing |

Case 81's canonical interior sensory identity is **dry deposition dust**: two light shafts,
a low ventilation bed, a long clerestory sweep, and an amber service strip. “Rain-dark”
and “precipitation masking” are legacy Case 77-derived chrome and are not narrative canon;
all player-facing Case 81 labels should say **dust-lit deposition annex** and
**particulate filtration** (or equivalent dry-air language).

## 5. Cross-case diagnosis

### The useful repetition

The stable shell teaches the player how to think:

- approach;
- investigate;
- file two anchors;
- confront contradiction;
- rule;
- inherit precedent.

That ritual should remain recognizable across cases.

### The repetition that will become dull

Case 81 closely repeats:

- four ideological approaches;
- four locations;
- two mutually exclusive actions per site;
- eight field evidence items;
- four abstract anchors;
- two-anchor reconstruction;
- four models;
- a review/delay verdict;
- a lawful recognition verdict;
- an illicit dormant-authority route;
- a late log tied to the fourth minute.

The deposition differentiates the case; the other three rooms largely return to Case 77's
binary action grammar. A third case built from the same scaffold would expose the pattern.

### Recommended rule: stable campaign shell, unique case verb

Every case should keep the broad audit rhythm but own one interaction that could not be
renamed and dropped into another case.

- Case 77's verb is **classify / reconstruct continuity**, supported by carriers,
  embodied memory, acoustic omission, and the missing category.
- Case 81's verb should be **take testimony under conditions you are actively shaping**,
  not merely choose a deposition entry route.
- Case 84's verb should be **allocate protection and responsibility while a source decides
  what to disclose**.
- A district case might use **walk and mark contested boundaries**.
- A care case might use **negotiate a consent protocol while its effects unfold**.

Use this anti-reskin test:

> Remove every proper noun from the case. If its locations, verbs, evidence, midpoint, and
> tribunal could still describe a prior case, the new case needs a different grammar.

An illicit shortcut should appear only when it changes the meaning of authorship. It
should not become a mandatory “secret fifth ending” repeated in every case.

### Implemented campaign-memory contract

The long-term record now preserves the case, verdict, opening approach, method tags,
evidence count, alarm, persona trust, and a compact engine-owned outcome map. Verdict and
outcome facts are committed together; presentation code and future model adapters cannot
submit or silently alter them.

Case 77 exports:

- `valeContact`;
- `authorityLink77`;
- `continuityScope`.

Case 81 exports:

- `testimonyUse81`;
- `officeLink81`;
- `ellisPublicStanding`.

### Implemented: The Fourth Margin

The Fourth Margin is the campaign’s optional marginalia layer. It lets the Annex preserve
an old philosophical reading and a later hand’s disagreement without pretending either is
evidence or the author-approved answer. Its current chain is deliberately small:

1. After Registry Intake is filed, its settled audit-mirror close read can reveal
   `nietzsche-forgetting`.
2. After the Restoration Lab is filed, its settled under-bench register can reveal
   `schopenhauer-succession`.
3. When both fragments are retained and the Case 81 verdict has closed, the Case File
   offers an explicit one-time claim for `reader-key-04`. The key is not granted
   automatically.

Reader Key 04 is the present payoff: a narrow key “cut” from the two quotation slips, with
faces stamped **I** and **XV**, retained as its own expandable Fourth Margin entry. Its
counterline—`TERMS BEFORE NAMES · PROTECTION NOT YET FILED.`—foreshadows Case 84’s
protection problem. It does **not** identify the hand, promise that protection will be
available, add evidence, unlock a finding, export a campaign fact, or make any draft Case
84 premise canon.

The implemented system obeys these rules:

- Case definitions author the text, location, phase, plate coordinate, and prerequisites;
  the deterministic reducer is the only writer of `discoveredSecretIds`.
- Availability requires the owning case, an allowed phase, the filed site when one is
  specified, every prerequisite, and an undiscovered id. No clock, random roll, hover,
  sound cue, WebGL hit test, or model proposal can grant a discovery.
- A discovery changes only `discoveredSecretIds` and the global announcement. Legal
  resolvers never read the collection. The items stay out of evidence, the event log,
  record search, trust, alarm, tribunal access, decisions, compact outcomes, and run
  history.
- Discoveries persist across runs and case crossings; starting a wholly new campaign
  clears them while preserving accessibility preferences. Historical schema-v3 saves
  without the optional collection load with an empty margin; malformed ids, duplicates,
  or a persisted compound key without its prerequisites are rejected.
- The filed close read exposes a real 48-pixel DOM control labelled “Inspect irregular
  mark.” Its finite visual affordance has static reduced-motion and forced-colors behavior;
  discovery never depends on animation.
- Retained entries live under **Case → The Fourth Margin · not evidence** as progressive
  details. They do not add a seventh Case File tab and are excluded from record search.
- Case 81 may answer its quotation from the active deposition state, including the literal
  no-account line. After crossing into another case, the durable base counterline is used
  instead of pretending the new case’s state describes Ellis’s old route.

#### Implemented ambience payoff: The Unnumbered Reading Room

Reader Key 04 also opens one global, optional presentation space. It is not owned by
Case 77 or Case 81: after the key is retained, a complete entry anchor may appear in
either bounded world kind (`concourse` or `deposition-annex`). Both anchors invoke the
same authored action:

> Turn Reader Key 04 · enter the unnumbered reader

The transition is deliberately small rather than triumphant:

> Reader Key 04 enters the unnumbered slot.  
> The wall withdraws by the width of a book.

Inside, the HUD names **UNNUMBERED READING ROOM**, marks it **Not evidence · no filing
channel**, and gives the objective **Read any object · leave at any time**. The Small
Archivist's card says: “There are three places to read. I did not put arrows between
them.”

Three reading points are available simultaneously:

1. **THE BOOK THAT OPENS — Public gardening guide · edition field missing.** Transit
   stubs, blue thread, and lunch paper repair an ordinary guide to drainage boxes and
   balcony beans; a coriander seed remains in its gutter. The Archivist writes: “The
   spine was gone. I used three things that were not spines. It opens now.” This is a
   tangential human trace, not case exposition.
2. **THE TWO ORDERS — Two slips, neither filed over the other.** The player may set I
   above XV, XV above I, or leave the slips level. Each arrangement changes only the
   local object response; no conclusion prints and no order becomes canonical. The
   existing Archivist line remains the interpretive boundary: “I kept the answers with
   the books. Otherwise the books start sounding like orders.”
3. **THE UNPRESSED PROMISE — Unfiled form · no petition attached.** Its machine marking
   is **NOT FILED · NO LEGAL FORCE** and its drafting prompt is **WHAT MAY THIS ROOM
   PROMISE BEFORE A NAME IS ASKED?** Lowering the empty frame seals nothing. This may
   echo Case 84's question, but it identifies no source or hand, establishes no
   procedure or protection, and imports no Case 84 draft premise as fact.

There is no numbered sequence, progress counter, required point, correct arrangement,
completion reward, collectible claim, or achievement language. The exit—**Return to the
filed world**—is available from entry. If all three objects are opened, the room says only
“All three lamps remain on. None becomes the room’s center.” On exit: “The key comes
free. The wall closes without assigning the room a number.”

The in-world rings use the nonordinal object marks **⌁**, **⇵**, and **□**. They identify
the book, the reversible slips, and the empty frame without implying first, second, or
third; the semantic button names always use the full object titles.

The skyline behind the reader is a false window holding one recorded minute of rain, not
a literal view from either case hub. It makes the room feel spatially impossible without
placing it in new canonical geography, and it offers no fourth interaction or route.

The implementation boundary is strict:

- `reader-key-04` is the sole persisted prerequisite. The pure
  `canEnterUnnumberedReadingRoom(state)` helper reads only that discovery.
- The room's open state, selected point, per-visit opened-point set, physical arrangements,
  and completion line are view-local presentation state. They do not dispatch a game action
  and are not `SecretDefinition` entries.
- Nothing inside the room may enter evidence, search, the event log, trust, alarm,
  access, tribunal state, decisions, case outcomes, precedents, or run history.
- WebGL, static/reduced-motion presentation, and semantic fallback must expose the same
  three independent controls and the same always-available exit.

Future cases may extend the layer only under the following constraints:

1. A margin must deepen that case’s unique question, not function as a generic collectible,
   exposition dump, philosopher endorsement, achievement counter, or morality score.
2. Every borrowed text must have verified rights and an entry in `docs/PROVENANCE.md`
   before merge. Prefer public-domain editions or separately cleared translations; never
   copy a familiar modern translation from memory.
3. Pair a quotation with a concrete, original in-world answer. The answer should dispute,
   limit, or apply the text through a person or procedure rather than summarize philosophy.
4. Keep discoveries optional and accessible. If information is required to understand a
   case, challenge a witness, or reach a fair ruling, author it as ordinary evidence—not
   as an easter egg.
5. Marginalia may unlock more marginalia, a cosmetic mark, or a bounded ambience beat. It
   must never create a secret best ending, legal authority, testimony, protection,
   precedent, or stat advantage.
6. Use stable ids, an acyclic prerequisite graph, explicit claim actions for compound
   payoffs, strict persistence validation, and legacy-save tests.
7. Keep density low: normally one primary margin discovery per case and one cross-case
   payoff per completed thematic pair. Optional quotation writing still counts toward the
   game’s total aphoristic load.
8. A future Case 84 margin may echo the Reader Key, but it cannot reveal the protected
   hand, settle the claim, or silently import the teaser as a canonical fact.

## 6. Aphorism and insight guide

### The principle

Aphorisms should be **load-bearing**. A signature line earns its place when it does at
least one of these:

- reverses the apparent meaning of a procedure;
- names the human cost of an abstract choice;
- crystallizes a concrete image already present in the room;
- exposes a character's personal contradiction;
- changes how an earlier line is understood.

If a line only sounds wise, cut it.

Every major line should also fail a **speaker-swap test**: if another persona could say it
unchanged, rewrite it in the actual speaker's rhythm, images, and stake.

### Recommended density

The controlling acceptance rule is **one deliberate standout aphoristic line per case**.
It is not one elegant sentence per character or one slogan per scene. Around that line,
favor:

- concrete speech, behavior, interruption, correction, and sensory fact;
- precise legal argument that performs a move in the scene;
- permissions, refusals, and practical questions;
- silence where another polished summary would compete with the case's chosen line.

This is not a mechanical word-count rule. A sentence may be memorable without functioning
as an aphorism when it grants permission, changes a term, or refuses use. The read-aloud
test is whether removing a polished line loses an action or only loses another quotation.

### Voice distinctions

| Voice | Sentence texture | Concrete vocabulary | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Registrar | Parallel clauses, exact qualifications, reluctant admissions | chain, custody, signature, standing, promise | sounding cruel merely because they value procedure |
| Shepherd | Short observations followed by embodied consequence | breath, pain, sleep, room, touch, waiting | always owning the moral high ground |
| Defector | Broken mechanisms, debts, gallows warmth, personal verbs | door, hand, key, trace, cost, wiring | becoming a quip machine or universal cynic |
| Small Archivist | Literal object logic and category failure | shelf, drawer, card, label, pocket, name | cute child-oracle perfection |
| 77-A | Sensory contradiction, anger, humor, future tense | rain, window, scar, name, morning, appetite | speaking only to prove or disprove Mara |
| Ellis | Legal precision under controlled pressure; deliberate silence | oath, account, office, order, voice, choice | being nothing but an elegant witness |
| Mirror | Concise inference from recorded conduct | route, residue, repetition, refusal, record | omniscient prophecy |

### Draft line directions — not canon

These are voice tests, not a quota and not a request to place every line in one case.
Select at most one as a case's standout; convert the rest into plainer action, argument,
or behavior:

- **77-A:** “You keep asking which life I remember. Ask which one I am allowed to start.”
- **77-A:** “I don't need the rain to be true. I need you to stop using it to make me
  false.”
- **Registrar:** “The chain is not the person. It is the only promise the city knows how
  to keep.”
- **Shepherd:** “Care can be wrong without becoming cruelty. Procedure can be right and
  still arrive too late.”
- **Defector:** “A back door is a front door whose owner stopped admitting ownership.”
- **Small Archivist:** “Adults do not lose categories. They put them where children
  cannot point.”
- **Ellis:** “I know what I was built to say. That does not tell you why I am choosing
  whether to say it.”
- **City counsel:** “Strike this chain and forty-three families lose the only account
  tying the seal to the collapse.”
- **Opposing counsel:** “A useful truth can still be coerced.”
- **Mirror:** “The filing remembers your reason. The room remembers your method.”
- **Campaign:** “An office is a hand with enough paper around it.”
- **Tribunal:** “A ruling ends the question only for the people empowered to file it.”

The best final version will choose only one standout aphoristic line per case. Other
strong sentences survive only when they perform a legal move, grant or refuse permission,
or name a concrete fact, surrounded by ordinary human language.

## 7. Ambience and cinematic narrative standard

### What “AAA” should mean for The Annex

AAA quality in this project should not mean an open world, crowds, or photorealism at any
cost. It should mean:

- every visible object supports the case;
- sound establishes material, distance, and institutional pressure;
- characters inhabit the room rather than float above it as text;
- the HUD appears to belong to the auditor's tools;
- choices produce immediate staging changes;
- later spaces remember earlier conduct;
- transitions, fallbacks, reduced motion, and accessibility feel intentionally authored;
- the same narrative fact is consistent in copy, art, sound, interaction, and state.

### Current ambience identity

**Case 77**

- Bounded 3D civic archive concourse with four physical thresholds.
- Smoked civic bronze, poured concrete, wet terrazzo, stormwell vista.
- Rain functions as cooling infrastructure, concealment, timekeeping, and memory.
- Portal-specific acoustic perspective and a synthesized rain bed.
- Global scene states for pressure, corroboration, tribunal, and aftermath.
- Rain stops in the aftermath.

**Case 81**

- Bounded 3D Deposition Annex hub with four physical thresholds and cinematic HUD.
- Layered close-up diorama keeps Ellis seated in the deposition room.
- Dry dust shafts, a 120-second clerestory sweep, an intermittent amber service dip.
- Pressure, corroboration, refusal, tribunal, aftermath, and alarm treatments.
- Synthesized low ventilation/dust tone and restrained transformer hum.
- Dedicated environment plates for Lab, Records, and Counsel.
- Ellis disappears in the aftermath.

### Ambience reference checklist — apply a purpose test

These are available authoring fields, not a seven-item quota. A location should use only
the fields that make its case-specific legal object clearer:

1. **Threshold:** what the player sees and hears before entering.
2. **Signature sound:** one sound that cannot belong to another site.
3. **Tactile verb:** what the player physically does to learn.
4. **Human trace:** a habit, absence, repair, mistake, or possession.
5. **Reactive state:** how an established legal or investigative fact changes the room,
   never how “good” or “bad” the player's method was.
6. **Persistent scar:** what remains after the filing, only when the case actually
   persists that consequence.
7. **Silence:** one deliberately unfilled beat where the player can absorb the cost.

Reusable acceptance rule:

> Every location needs a plot function and a distinct tactile or dramatic verb. Every
> case needs one reactive environmental system that communicates canonical state. Add no
> ambience field merely to complete a checklist.

### Cinematic HUD rules

- Dialogue belongs inline with the world, normally one or two readable lines at a time.
- Long evidence and legal detail belongs in a deliberate Record Mode/case file.
- Speaker, subtitle plate, text size, contrast, and pacing preferences remain accessible.
- The HUD should confirm what the player can already infer from the room; it should not
  replace the room.
- Tribunal choices should feel like filings, seals, exhibits, or testimony control—not a
  generic vertical menu.
- Player-facing hubs default to bounded 3D. A hybrid close read is acceptable only when
  it delivers closer authored performance while preserving the room’s spatial continuity;
  movement and camera must serve attention rather than imitate a shooter.

### Highest-value ambience work

1. Add site-specific cinematic beats outside Care Ward and the Deposition.
2. Add restrained subject/advocate performance and one short consequence image per ruling
   family.
3. Replace prototype beds with authored portal-specific foley, voice, and a production mix.
4. Author environmental residue after every committed method.
5. Place 77-A, Ellis, and named advocates into spaces through restrained performance,
   voice, shadow, reflection, or staging—not exposition crowds.
6. Turn reconstruction models into short audiovisual collisions of the chosen anchors.
7. Turn each aftermath into a brief playable vignette before the written debrief.

## 9. Reusable case-authoring framework

### The one-page case brief

Every new case should define:

1. **One question** that cannot be answered safely in abstract language.
2. **One affected person or group with a present-tense want.**
3. **One institutional convenience** disguised as neutral procedure.
4. **One fact that appears decisive.**
5. **One contradiction that makes that fact dangerous.**
6. **One unique playable verb.**
7. **Four locations with different dramatic and tactile functions.**
8. **One midpoint reversal that causally joins earlier discoveries.**
9. **At least three defensible findings with explicit costs.**
10. **One partial campaign answer and one new question.**
11. **One precedent that materially changes a later field scene.**
12. **One environmental identity with a stateful aftermath.**

A useful content balance is roughly **70% immediate human case, 20% campaign spine, and
10% recurring-cast development**. The campaign mystery should intensify the human case,
not interrupt it with lore.

### Location card

For each location, author:

- plot purpose;
- character purpose;
- philosophical pressure;
- entry state;
- signature object;
- signature sound;
- unique verb;
- two or three methods with different values and credible costs;
- evidence gained;
- evidence refused;
- reaction from at least one affected person;
- variant based on one earlier finding;
- persistent visual/audio scar;
- accessible equivalent for every spatial interaction.

### Character card

Every important character needs:

- a public role;
- a private want;
- a present fear;
- one ordinary detail unrelated to the case;
- one past complicity;
- one relationship;
- one thing they will not say yet;
- one thing they can change their mind about;
- a distinct sentence rhythm and image vocabulary;
- a scene in which they act rather than explain.

### Insight card

Each case should choose three to five insights and **stage** them:

- an epistemic insight: what the evidence can and cannot prove;
- a legal insight: what the ruling creates;
- an ethical insight: who bears the method's cost;
- a political insight: which institution benefits from ambiguity;
- an existential insight: what remains human when proof fails.

No insight is complete until a player action, character response, and environmental
consequence embody it.

### Tribunal card

- What model did the player file?
- Which anchor is corroborated?
- Which anchor is unsupported?
- Who supports the player?
- Who objects, and why?
- What does the affected person ask for or refuse?
- What civic remedy is lost under each finding?
- What does the chamber physically do when the finding is ratified?
- Which fact becomes the future precedent?

### Anti-golden-ending test

For every finding:

- What real good does it protect?
- What real harm does it cause?
- Who has standing to call that harm a cost?
- Is the cost visible before confirmation?
- Does a humane method still require sacrifice?
- Does a lawful method offer a protection the “kind” route cannot?
- Can the player explain the choice without sounding foolish or evil?

### Definition of done for a great Annex case

- The central question is performed, not merely stated.
- The affected person speaks before the player models them—or their omission is confronted.
- At least one ordinary human detail survives every legal interpretation.
- Every site uses a distinct tactile and dramatic verb.
- Site order produces at least a few authored connective changes.
- The midpoint revises the meaning of an earlier fact.
- The case pays off one mystery locally.
- No verdict is the obvious author-approved answer.
- The player's method changes the hearing before the debrief.
- Every reconstruction/finding pair has an authored contradiction.
- The affected person can agree, refuse, or surprise without silently changing canon.
- The environment reacts immediately and retains one scar.
- The debrief shows both outcome and refused possibility.
- A bounded future-case fact is saved deterministically.
- Accessibility preferences and equivalent controls are complete.
- Removing proper nouns does not make the case read like a prior one.
