## VERDICT SUMMARY

**Is gameplay smooth?** Yes. The whole spine works end-to-end with zero console errors across a full
run and Case 81 entry. The core loop (concourse → enter room → arm a method → confirm → consequence
beat → case file) is coherent, the arm-then-confirm pattern is consistent everywhere, save/resume
works (resumed mid-tribunal after a reload), and each of the four sites is a genuinely distinct
interaction (witness room, carrier-seating rail, sensor-alley, archive vault). Friction points are
localized, not systemic.

**Is text volume right for an ambience-first game?** In the diorama scenes, yes — the room leads and
the text is a thin caption layer (Care-ward room = 155 words, most of it optional). The problem is
the **three full-screen editorial phases** (reconstruction 279 words, tribunal 438 words, debrief 322
words) where the diorama disappears and prose takes the whole viewport. That is a deliberate register
shift, but it is where the developer's "too much text" worry is real: the tribunal makes you scroll
past ~1,350px of hero + hearing prose to reach the decision. This is a *hierarchy/placement* problem,
not a word-count one (per your own memory note).

**Does the UI teach itself?** Partially — and this is the weakest area. The strong teachers are the
per-state hint line ("Select a threshold in the world" / "Choose one marked method"), the inline
"— select again to file" on armed choices, and the visible `Alt C` / `Alt E` chips. But three things
a new player must discover have **no teaching or a misleading one**: (1) the `01`/`02` numbered action
ordinals imply number-key shortcuts that **do not exist**; (2) the tribunal's verdict cards are
**disabled until a "legal reach" radio is set**, with no visible explanation of the dependency; and
(3) the coded first-run "two-sites budget" rule **did not surface** in the first room I entered — the
player instead learns the limit reactively by hitting it on site 3. A player can learn to play without
a manual, but they will mis-learn the number keys and be briefly stuck at the tribunal.

## WHAT WORKS (protect these — do not regress)

1. **Arm-then-confirm on every irreversible choice is consistent and legible.** First click arms and
   appends "— select again to file" + "— final for this run"; a second click files. Verified live in
   the Care room, Registry room, and Tribunal. The finality is reinforced in up to four places at once
   (objective, hotspot label, filing-review plate, action prompt). (`ChoiceButton.tsx:136,143`)
2. **The diorama truly leads in the scenes.** Care-ward room body text is 155 words and the two method
   hotspots are visible amber/white rings on the photograph; the figure behind the rain glass carries
   the mood. This is the ambience-first intent landing.
3. **Consequence feedback is immediate and diegetic.** After filing, a subtitle beat plays over the
   scene, then a bottom bar shows "Evidence admitted: … · Standing: The Shepherd +2, The Small
   Archivist +1" with portraits. Trust deltas are shown in-fiction, not as a stats HUD.
4. **Progressive disclosure via drawers is excellent.** The Case file (Case/Ledger/Evidence/Log/People/
   Search tabs, 48px tab height) and Location detail drawers hold all the deep lore (the ~120-word
   record blocks) OUT of the scene, opt-in. This is exactly how to keep the scene clean.
5. **The "omitted site" concept is taught by contact.** Visiting site 3/4 after the 2-site budget is
   spent shows "Filing budget reached · inspect this location or continue to the memory lattice" and
   "2 sites omitted from the filed record" — a clean, in-world explanation.
6. **Campaign continuity pays off.** Case 81 opens with a teal "Residual signal" quoting my Case 77
   verdict ("Last run you gave her Mara's name back… Ask her whether it still fits"). Strong.
7. **Accessibility surface is real.** Reduce motion / High contrast / Larger text / Show trust values
   toggles on the title screen; a "Skip to current scene" skip link; `aria-keyshortcuts` on Alt C/E;
   reduced-motion blanket-kills animations (`cinematicHud.css:811-820`). Mobile correctly drops the
   `Alt C`/`Alt E` chips (no keyboard).
8. **Mobile stacks without breaking.** The HUD reflows to a single column, type stays legible, method
   rings are 44px with 15px spacing (both pass), and no horizontal scroll. No layout collapse.

## FINDINGS (ranked; P0 blocks comprehension, P1 hurts flow, P2 polish)

### F1 — Numbered `01`/`02` action ordinals imply keyboard shortcuts that don't exist. `P1`
- **Screen:** every scene HUD action list (concourse "01 Enter…", room "01 Location detail / 02 Return…",
  tribunal, debrief). Captures 04, 05, 12, 17.
- **Observed:** dispatching a `Digit1` keydown on the concourse did nothing (site did not enter).
  Source confirms: the `01`/`02` are `aria-hidden` decorative ordinals with **no digit-key handler
  anywhere** (`CinematicHud.tsx:270-272`; grep for `Digit1`/`'1'` returns none). VERIFIED (live: key
  press inert; code: no handler).
- **Best-practice violated:** ui-ux-pro-max `ux/Accessibility/Keyboard Navigation` ("all functionality
  accessible via keyboard; tab order matches visual order") and the general affordance principle that
  a leading numeral in an action list reads as a hotkey (Miller-column / command-palette convention).
  The label advertises an affordance the app doesn't honor.
- **Recommendation:** either (a) wire digit keys 1–4 to the visible actions (cheapest win — the actions
  are already an ordered list in `Investigation.tsx`), or (b) if the numerals are purely ornamental,
  drop them or restyle them so they don't read as key caps. Given the game already teaches `Alt C`/
  `Alt E` as real chips, honoring 1/2 would make the whole HUD keyboard-consistent. Pointer:
  `src/components/CinematicHud.tsx:270-272`, handler home `src/components/CinematicHud.tsx:112-146`.

### F2 — Tribunal verdicts are `disabled` until a "legal reach" radio is set, with no visible reason. `P1`
- **Screen:** Tribunal. Captures 17 (+ measured).
- **Observed:** the three verdict cards (`Certify… / Charter… / Order protected review`) render with
  `disabled=""` and `aria-pressed=false`; clicking them is inert. They only enable after one of the
  `individual` / `general` reach radios is chosen (VERIFIED live: selecting `individual` flipped
  `verdictEnabled` true). The objective line ("Set the legal reach of this ruling before the finding is
  filed") is the *only* signal, and it sits ~1,350px above the cards; the disabled cards themselves give
  no inline "choose a reach first" hint.
- **Best-practice violated:** ui-ux-pro-max `ux/Forms/Progressive disclosure` + `ux/Feedback` (a
  blocked control must state what unblocks it) and Nielsen "visibility of system status." A disabled
  primary action with no adjacent cause is a classic dead-end.
- **Recommendation:** when no reach is selected, show an inline helper on/above the verdict group
  ("Select a legal reach above to issue a finding") and/or visually tie the reach radios to the
  findings (shared container, arrow, or a "Step 1 / Step 2" label). Keep the cards disabled but make
  the dependency legible. Pointer: `src/components/Tribunal.tsx` (reach radios render just above
  `Issue a finding` H2; verdict `choice-row`s are the disabled buttons).

### F3 — The coded first-run "two-sites budget" rule did not surface for a new player. `P1`
- **Screen:** first room entered on a cleared save (Care ward 12). Captures 05.
- **Observed:** on a freshly cleared save, entering the first site, the room objective is "Choose one
  method in the room. This location then closes." and **none** of the first-run rule strings
  ("record of two sites", "two are yours to leave outside the filed record", "two sites") appear in the
  live DOM. Source shows this rule exists, gated on `completedSites.length === 0`
  (`Investigation.tsx:~1791`), but it did not render in the observed scene-first Care-ward path.
  Net effect: the player learns the 2-of-4 filing budget **reactively**, only when they hit it at site 3
  ("filing budget reached / 2 sites omitted"). VERIFIED (live absence in first room); UNVERIFIED whether
  it renders in some other phase/inspector view I did not trigger — needs developer confirmation.
- **Best-practice violated:** ui-ux-pro-max `ux/Onboarding` (teach the core constraint before the player
  can violate it) — the 2-site budget is the single most consequential rule of a run and is currently
  taught after the fact.
- **Recommendation:** confirm whether the `Investigation.tsx:1791` block is reachable in the scene-first
  flow; if not, surface a one-line, self-retiring diegetic note on the first concourse or first room
  ("The tribunal will hear two sites. The other two are yours to leave off the record."). See Onboarding
  Hints §H1.

### F4 — Full-screen editorial phases invert the text:scene ratio; the decision is far below the fold. `P1`
- **Screen:** Reconstruction (279 words), Tribunal (438 words), Debrief (322 words). Captures 16, 17, 18.
- **Observed:** these three phases drop the diorama for long single-column prose. On the tribunal the
  first verdict card sits ~1,350px below the top; the player scrolls past a tall hero ("The record is
  sufficient. It is not complete."), a subject-hearing quote, and the reach radios before seeing the
  decision. Measured word counts are 3–5× the scene rooms.
- **Best-practice violated:** your own memory scar ("'too much text' is a hierarchy/placement problem,
  not an area one") + Nielsen "recognition over recall" — the actionable control should be reachable
  without hunting. Not an accessibility failure, a flow one.
- **Recommendation:** keep the prose but pull the decision into view — e.g. a sticky "Issue finding"
  affordance, or collapse the hero to a band once the player scrolls, or a "Jump to findings" anchor
  (the amber "Review findings ↓" button hints at this but doesn't scroll-lock the decision into reach).
  For reconstruction/debrief, consider a two-column layout at ≥1024px so the anchor list and the action
  are co-visible. Pointers: `src/components/Tribunal.tsx`, `src/components/Reconstruction.tsx`,
  `src/components/Debrief.tsx`.

### F5 — Scene hotspots have no rest-state pulse; on touch they lose their name labels entirely. `P2`
- **Screen:** all rooms; acute on mobile. Captures 05, 22.
- **Observed:** hotspot / scene-zone rings are static (1.5px ring + center dot); the only motion is a
  hover/focus transition, and **no `@keyframes` pulse** drives them at rest
  (`styles.css:8347-8376`, `SceneZone.tsx:77` + `styles.css:10866-10906`). At `max-width:460px` the
  `.scene-hotspot-label` opacity is forced to 0 and only returns on `:hover`/`:focus-visible`
  (`styles.css:8473-8485`) — which touch devices never fire. Live-measured on mobile: the two Deposition-
  suite method rings are exactly **44×44px** (minimum, not comfortable) sitting 15px apart, both showing
  the same generic action label rather than their distinct method names — so which ring is which method
  is ambiguous until tapped. (The desktop rings are findable; this is a touch-specific degradation.)
- **Best-practice violated:** ui-ux-pro-max `ux/Touch/Touch Target Size` (44px is the floor, not a
  target — aim larger for primary scene interactions) and `ux/Feedback/Empty States` / discoverability
  (an interactive mark should read as interactive at rest without hover).
- **Recommendation:** (a) add a subtle rest-state pulse to the *recommended* hotspot only (respecting
  `prefers-reduced-motion`, which already zeroes durations) so first-timers see "this is where the
  choice is"; (b) on touch, keep a short always-on label (even just the method name) instead of hiding
  it; (c) bump the mobile ring hit area above 44px for the primary method marks. Pointers:
  `src/scene/SceneStage.tsx:199-238`, `src/cinematicHud.css`/`styles.css:8333-8485` and `10866-10906`.

### F6 — Concourse thresholds A/C/D are unlabeled circles at rest; only the suggested one is named. `P2`
- **Screen:** Concourse. Captures 04.
- **Observed:** all four world portals are 48×48px with correct `aria-label`s ("Enter Registry intake",
  etc.), but visually only the suggested threshold (B/E) shows its name; the others are bare lettered
  circles until hover/selection reveals a tooltip. A new player sees one named door and three anonymous
  ones, and may not realize A/C/D are enterable sites (the objective "You may choose any route" and the
  hint "Select a threshold in the world" carry the load).
- **Best-practice violated:** ui-ux-pro-max `ux/Navigation/Active State` + discoverability — available
  destinations should be identifiable, not hover-gated (and hover doesn't exist on touch).
- **Recommendation:** show a persistent short label under each threshold at ≥tablet, or at least reveal
  all four on first-run for one beat. Low effort, meaningfully clarifies the hub. Pointer:
  `annex-world-portal` in `src/components/Investigation.tsx` / `cinematicHud.css`.

### F7 — Route/verdict selection is a two-step (select card, then a separate primary button). `P2`
- **Screen:** Briefing route cards + Reconstruction. Captures 03, 16.
- **Observed:** clicking a route card ("Begin with the person") does not advance; a separate "Begin
  audit →" button confirms. Same for reconstruction (select 2 anchors → "File …model →"). This is
  internally consistent with the arm-then-confirm philosophy, but on the briefing it's momentarily
  unclear that a second click on a different control is required (the card gives no "selected, now
  confirm" affordance beyond styling).
- **Best-practice violated:** minor `ux/Feedback` (selection state should point to its confirm).
- **Recommendation:** on card-select, nudge focus to / pulse the "Begin audit" button, or label it
  "Begin with the person →" once a route is chosen, so the hand-off is explicit. Low priority — it
  resolves on the second look.

### F8 — Finality copy is repeated up to four times simultaneously on the armed state. `P2`
- **Screen:** armed method in a room. Captures 06.
- **Observed:** when a method is armed, "final for this run" appears in the objective, the hotspot label,
  the bottom-left filing-review plate, AND the bottom-right action prompt at once. For an irreversible
  action this reinforcement is defensible, but it's the densest redundancy in the game and slightly
  competes with the scene.
- **Best-practice violated:** mild `ux/Forms` over-communication; not a defect, a tuning note.
- **Recommendation:** keep the warning in one primary place (the action prompt) and demote the others to
  a single quiet echo. Judgment call — leave as-is if playtests show players miss the finality.

## ONBOARDING HINTS — concrete, diegetic proposals (what you most asked for)

The civic-archive fiction gives you a perfect excuse to teach through the institution's own voice
(objectives, stamped notices, the Mirror, persona asides) instead of tutorial popups. Ranked by impact:

- **H1 (fixes F3) — A stamped "intake notice" on the first concourse.** Before the first entry, print
  one self-retiring line in the mandate plate, in the Authority's register: *"Filing budget: this
  tribunal hears two sites. The remaining two are yours to inspect and leave off the record."* Gate it
  on `completedSites.length === 0` so it disappears after the first filing (the code already intends
  this — it just isn't surfacing). This teaches the single most important rule *before* it can be
  violated, in-world.

- **H2 (fixes F1) — Teach the number keys by using them, or stop advertising them.** If you wire digit
  keys, add them to the same chip vocabulary as Alt C/E: render the `01` ordinal as a real `<kbd>1</kbd>`
  cap. If you don't, replace the numerals with a neutral bullet so nobody reaches for a dead key. The
  fiction ("press the numbered channel") supports either.

- **H3 (fixes F2) — Make the tribunal a visible two-step ritual.** Frame the reach radios as
  *"Step 1 · Set the ruling's reach"* and the verdict cards as *"Step 2 · Issue the finding (unlocks
  once reach is set)."* Diegetically: the Auditor "cannot enter a finding until the reach is declared."
  A single greyed line on the disabled cards — *"Declare a reach above to issue a finding"* — removes
  the dead-end without a popup.

- **H4 (fixes F5/F6, diegetic hotspot teaching) — Let the room "point" once.** On first entry to any
  scene, have the recommended mark breathe a slow amber pulse and surface its label for ~2s ("the file
  the Authority expects you to open first"), then settle to rest. This reads as the archive's lighting
  guiding attention, not a UI coach-mark, and respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (which already zeroes
  it). On touch, keep the label persistent.

- **H5 (arm/confirm reassurance) — one-time "the record is provisional" aside.** The first time a player
  arms a method, let the site persona say it in character (e.g. the Shepherd: *"Nothing's filed until
  you say it twice."*). This teaches the confirm step through voice rather than the current sr-only-plus-
  inline text, and deepens ambience.

All five are text/CSS-level and stay inside the ratified identity (dark civic-noir, amber accent, room
leads) — no new UI chrome, no modal tutorials.
